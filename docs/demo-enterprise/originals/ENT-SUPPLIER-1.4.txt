Controlled working reference; illustrative procedures, not financial advice.

1. Purpose and scope
1.1 This procedure separates supplier selection, commercial approval, purchase authorization and payment validation. It applies to fictional purchases of services, equipment and software. The procedure avoids promising a spend threshold that has not been approved in the controlled delegation schedule.
1.2 A purchase request states the business need, scope, expected delivery, information access and proposed owner. An existing supplier relationship does not remove the need to approve a materially different service. The requester identifies any personal interest so the selection owner can arrange an independent review.

2. Supplier intake
2.1 Collect the supplier's registered name, service description, contact and approved tax or billing identifiers through the controlled intake form. Do not collect personal identity documents in a general project folder. Finance validates payment details through the approved verification channel rather than trusting an attachment forwarded from an unknown address.
2.2 A proposed bank-detail change is treated as a new validation event. The payment team contacts the known supplier representative using an independently verified route. Preserve the request, verification outcome and reviewer. Urgency or an executive name in an email is not sufficient evidence to skip this check.

3. Review responsibilities
Review area | Responsible owner | Required output
Business need | Requesting department | Scope and delivery acceptance plan
Information access | Security and information owner | Approved handling and access conditions
Contract terms | Legal operations | Reviewed agreement or deviation note
Payment setup | Finance operations | Verified billing and payment record
Purchase authorization | Delegated approver | Decision in controlled purchase system

3.1 The department records service acceptance independently from invoice approval. An invoice cannot prove that a deliverable was received. Where a service is recurring, the owner records the service period and any discrepancy before authorizing payment.
3.2 A contract deviation identifies the clause, proposed language, reason and legal reviewer. A supplier's marked-up draft is not an approved agreement. Keep the original received file and the accepted version, with dates and the matter reference needed to reconstruct the negotiation.

4. Ordering and changes
4.1 Issue a purchase order after the required reviews and delegation check. The order identifies the agreed scope and delivery location. A requester must not split a single requirement into several requests to avoid a control. A scope change is reviewed against the original approval before commitments are made.
4.2 During delivery, the business owner records accepted items, open issues and dependencies. A missed date is escalated with evidence rather than silently changing the agreed schedule. The owner checks any proposed substitute for information handling, compatibility and contractual impact.
4.3 Cancellation identifies obligations already incurred, unaccepted deliveries and information access that must be withdrawn. Procurement coordinates with the business owner, legal team and service desk so a cancelled order does not leave a supplier account or shared document link active.

5. Reconciliation and closure
5.1 Finance matches the invoice to the purchase authorization and service acceptance. Differences are recorded and resolved before payment. The reviewer can see original quantities, approved changes and acceptance events without relying on a rewritten spreadsheet total.
5.2 At closure, the owner confirms final delivery, remaining warranty or support commitments and the disposition of supplier access. Keep a concise handover record for the support team. Contract records remain within the approved information-handling schedule rather than a guessed retention period.
