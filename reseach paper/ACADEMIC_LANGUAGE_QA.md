# Academic language quality checks

Reviewed the abstract and every body section for grammar, sentence variety, repetitive phrasing, paragraph purpose, terminology, citation support, method-to-results consistency and authorial voice. Incorporated the supplied editorial changes where they preserved the implementation. The major technical corrections are documented in the [scientific audit](HUMANIZATION_AUDIT.md) and [before-and-after review](BEFORE_AFTER_WRITING_REVIEW.md).

- The original and final clean prose each contain 42 paragraph blocks, including the abstract. Thirty-three body blocks were revised. The abstract was shortened from 189 to 160 words under the same word-count convention.
- The governing terms are now “strict path,” “related-passage path,” “effective interval,” and “authorized peers.” The results distinguish tested questions from disputes, and observed marker absence from a security guarantee.
- Word's local U.S. English proofing was applied to comparable continuous prose with headings, tables, citations and display math removed for the run. Approximate sentence counts use a punctuation-and-uppercase boundary rule. Both are diagnostic aids for technical prose.
- Word flagged 31 spelling and 6 grammar items before, and 30 spelling and 8 grammar items after. Flagged samples include proper names, technical identifiers, comma preferences and sentences distorted by formula removal. The sentence proxy counted 248 to 246 sentences, with a mean of 14.6 to 14.8 words and 3 to 4 sentences over 30 words. These counts do not measure academic quality.
- LanguageTool's standalone tool was identified, but a usable Java runtime was not available on PATH. Paperpal's free service was identified. The unpublished draft stayed in the local proofing workflow. No service-generated plagiarism or AI-detector score is claimed.

Author review should focus on the research contribution, assumptions about policy authority, the label-generation record and the exact disclosure text. The editorial pass does not establish human authorship.
